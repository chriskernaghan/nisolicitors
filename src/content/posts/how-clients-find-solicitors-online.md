Most people looking for a solicitor don't browse. They have a problem, they pull out their phone, and they search something like "conveyancing solicitor Belfast" or "family solicitor near me". What happens in the next thirty seconds decides whether your firm gets the call or the one down the road does. Here's what's actually going on behind that search, and the few things that genuinely move whether you show up.

## The map results matter more than your website

When someone searches for a solicitor in their town, the first thing they usually see isn't a list of websites. It's a little map with three firms pinned on it. Those three spots are the most valuable real estate in local search, and they're not controlled by your website at all. They're controlled by your Google Business Profile.

If your firm doesn't have a Business Profile, or has one that's half-filled-in and forgotten about, you're invisible in the exact spot where most people are looking. Setting one up is free, it takes an afternoon, and for a lot of firms it does more than any amount of website tinkering.

The things that help you appear in that map pack are fairly down-to-earth. A complete, accurate profile. Your firm's name, address and phone number written identically everywhere they appear online. Genuine reviews from real clients. And a profile that's actually kept active rather than abandoned.

> For a local firm, a well-kept Google Business Profile often beats a beautiful website that Google can't make sense of.

## Reviews do a surprising amount of work

In a profession built on trust, reviews carry real weight, and they pull double duty. They nudge you up the local rankings, and they reassure the nervous person deciding whether to ring you. You don't need hundreds. A steady trickle of honest reviews from happy clients, gathered over time, does more than a sudden burst.

The simplest approach is to just ask. When a matter wraps up well and a client is grateful, that's the moment. Most people are happy to leave a few words if you make it easy and ask at the right time.

## Your website still has a job

None of this means the website doesn't matter. It means the website's job is more specific than people assume.

Once someone has found you, on the map or through a search, your site has to do three things quickly. It has to confirm you handle their kind of matter, it has to feel trustworthy, and it has to make getting in touch effortless. A page for each practice area, written in plain language rather than legalese, helps with the first. Clear design and a few real details about your firm help with the second. An obvious phone number and a simple contact form handle the third.

What your website does not need to do is rank first for every legal term under the sun. Competing on broad, generic searches against the big national firms is usually a losing game for a high-street practice. The winnable ground is local and specific. Being the obvious choice for your town and your practice areas is worth far more than chasing traffic that was never going to become a client.

## Where to put your effort

If you're a local firm wondering where to start, the order is fairly clear. Sort your Google Business Profile first, because it's free and it's where the searches land. Gather reviews steadily, because they help you rank and help people choose you. And make sure your website, whatever state it's in, clearly states what you do and makes you easy to contact.

Get those three right and you've covered most of how clients actually find a firm like yours. The fancier stuff can wait until the basics are working.
